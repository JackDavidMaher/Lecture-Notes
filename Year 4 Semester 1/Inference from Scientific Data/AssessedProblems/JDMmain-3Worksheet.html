Bayes' Theorem¶

$$ P(\lambda|D) = \frac{P(D|\lambda) P(\lambda)}{P(D)} $$

Likelihood¶

$$ P(D|\lambda) = \prod_i^n P(x_i|\lambda) = \prod_i^n \lambda e^{-\lambda x_i} = \lambda^n e^{-\lambda \sum_i^n x_i} $$ $$ S = \sum_i^n x_i $$ $$ P(D|\lambda) = \lambda^n e^{-\lambda S}$$

Prior¶

$$ P(\lambda) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda} $$

Posterior¶

$$ P(\lambda|D) = \frac{P(D|\lambda) P(\lambda)}{P(D)} = \frac{(\lambda^n e^{-\lambda S})( \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda})}{P(D)} = \frac{\lambda^{n+\alpha-1} e^{-\lambda (S+\beta)}}{\Gamma(\alpha) P(D)} $$

Normalisation¶

$$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} P(\lambda|D) \, d\lambda = 1 $$ $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^{n+\alpha-1} e^{-\lambda (S+\beta)}}{\Gamma(\alpha) P(D)} \, d\lambda = 0 + \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^{n+\alpha-1} e^{-\lambda (S+\beta)}}{\Gamma(\alpha) P(D)} \, d\lambda = 1 $$ $$ \int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a} e^{bx} \, dx = \frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{(-b)^{a+1}} $$ $$ a = n + \alpha - 1, b = -(S+\beta) $$ $$ \int_{0}^{\infty} \lambda^{n+\alpha-1} e^{-\lambda (S+\beta)} \, d\lambda = \frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{(S+\beta)^{n+\alpha}} $$ $$ P(D) = \frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha) (S+\beta)^{n+\alpha}} $$ $$ P(\lambda|D) = \frac{(S+\beta)^{n+\alpha}}{\Gamma(n+\alpha)} \lambda^{n+\alpha-1} e^{-\lambda (S+\beta)} $$

Posterior¶

I used Bayes' Theorem to go from the likelihood and prior to the posterior. I did not write out the evidence ($P(D)$) explicitly as I knew that it was a normalisation constant that could be calculated in the next step.

Normalisation¶

I then normalised the posterior by integrating over all possible values of $\lambda$ and setting the integral equal to 1. The function was $0$ for $\lambda < 0$ as $\lambda$ represents a rate parameter so must positive. I used the standard integral formula $ \int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a} e^{bx} \, dx = \frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{(-b)^{a+1}} $ and substituted in the equivalent values for $a$ and $b$ to evaluate the integral and solve for the normalisation constant.

Essentially I set that integral $= \Gamma{\alpha} P(D)$ and rearranged for $P(D)$ explicitly.

Then I just plugged back into Bayes' Theorem to get the final normalised posterior.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from math import gamma
x = np.linspace(-5, 15, 100)
def gamma_pdf(x1, k1, theta):
    return np.where(x1>0, ((x1 ** (k1 - 1)) * np.exp(-x1 / theta)) / (gamma(k1) * theta ** k1), 0)
plt.plot(x, gamma_pdf(x, 1, 1), label=r'$(k_1,\theta)=(1,1)$')
plt.plot(x, gamma_pdf(x, 2, 1), label=r'$(k_1,\theta)=(2,1)$')
plt.plot(x, gamma_pdf(x, 2, 2), label=r'$(k_1,\theta)=(2,2)$')

plt.title('Question 2.1  -  Gamma PDFs')
plt.legend()
plt.show()
